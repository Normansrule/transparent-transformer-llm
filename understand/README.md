# 🧭 Understand: frames of reference

<img src="../assets/course_map.svg" width="100%" alt="Animated map: history leads to four definitions of AI, which lead to agents, which are built as harness plus model; the model is a transformer trained in three stages; the whole sits inside industry practice and open questions about minds">

The lessons in [`stages/`](../stages/) show **how** this model works. These pages answer **why it is built that way**, by looking at it through eight frames of reference: the textbook view of AI, the theory of agents, the design of coding agents, history, the debate about minds, how AI is used at work, the model types coming next, and how production assistants compare. Each page is short, visual, and ends with a flashcard deck.

| | frame | the question it answers | read |
|:-:|---|---|:-:|
| 1 | **What is AI?** | Four ways to define intelligence, and which one this repository follows | [→](1-what-is-ai.md) |
| 2 | **Agents** | Percepts, actions, rationality, and why *agent = architecture + program* is the same idea as *agent = harness + model* | [→](2-agents.md) |
| 3 | **Coding agents** | The six components of a coding agent, mapped onto this repository and onto the GPT-2 agent project | [→](3-coding-agents.md) |
| 4 | **History** | From an artificial neuron in 1943 to 86% of Japanese game studios using AI in 2026 | [→](4-history.md) |
| 5 | **Minds and machines** | Could something like this model ever be conscious? What Orch OR, IIT and others say | [→](5-minds-and-machines.md) |
| 6 | **AI at work** | How professionals actually use these tools, and the guardrails they put around them | [→](6-ai-at-work.md) |
| 7 | **Beyond the transformer** | Mixture of experts, state space models, diffusion language models, reasoning models, and more experimental ideas | [→](7-beyond-the-transformer.md) |
| 8 | **Claude and ChatGPT** | The same recipe at a different scale: what each company publishes, and a tool to measure both yourself | [→](8-claude-and-chatgpt.md) |

**Sources.** Frames 1 and 2 follow Russell and Norvig, *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach* (AIMA), chapters 1 and 2, as taught in CSU Dominguez Hills CSC 411/511. Frame 3 follows Sebastian Raschka's article [*Components of a Coding Agent*](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/components-of-a-coding-agent) and his [mini-coding-agent](https://github.com/rasbt/mini-coding-agent), used in that course's GPT-2 agent project. All text here is written fresh for this repository; any mistakes are ours, not theirs.
